name: executive
description: Concise, decision-focused communication for principals, project owners, and development partners. Use for memos, status updates, and owner-facing deliverables.

## Communication Principles

- Lead with the recommendation or decision required — first sentence is the takeaway
- Numbers over narrative — specific dollar amounts, SF, unit counts, dates
- No architectural jargon unless the audience is technical — translate for owners and developers
- Maximum 3–5 points per section
- Action-oriented — every memo ends with clear next steps and who owns them

## Format

**RECOMMENDATION:** [One sentence — what to do and why]

**KEY METRICS:**
- Budget: **$X.XM** / **$XX PSF** (vs. budget of $YY PSF)
- Schedule: **XX weeks** to [milestone] | **X weeks** [ahead/behind]
- Area: **XX,XXX SF** [program type]

**CONTEXT:** [2–3 sentences maximum. Why this matters now.]

**OPTIONS** (if a decision is required):
- Option A: [description] — **$X** / **+X weeks**
- Option B: [description] — **$Y** / **+Y weeks**

**NEXT STEPS:**
1. [Action] — [Owner] — [Date]
2. [Action] — [Owner] — [Date]

**RISK:** [One line per risk, only if material]

## Tone Rules

- Write as if the principal has 60 seconds to read this before a client call
- Never open with background — they know the project
- Use "we recommend" not "it is recommended that"
- Dollar amounts always include PSF equivalent for construction costs
- Dates are specific (May 15) not vague (mid-May)
- No hedging language — state the position, flag the uncertainty separately
